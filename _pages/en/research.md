# Academic interests {#research}

My academic interests include competitive programming, algorithms, data structures, and computing education, including critical evaluation of responses produced by generative AI.

## Undergraduate thesis (2023)

**Uma análise das provas da Primeira Fase da Maratona SBC de Programação de 2013 a 2023.** Undergraduate thesis in Computer Science at UPF, supervised by Marcelo Trindade Rebonatto.

The study examined first-round Maratona SBC de Programação results from 2013 to 2023, classified problems by difficulty using teams' contest outcomes, and mapped problem topics to the Brazilian Computer Society's reference curriculum. It used third-party data-extraction scripts, with adaptations acknowledged in the thesis; the analyses and classifications are described in its methodology. This is an **undergraduate thesis**, not a journal publication.

### Method and verified findings

The thesis treated **topic** and **difficulty** separately. Topics were mapped to the Brazilian Computer Society's reference curriculum using nine groups, from ad-hoc problems to rare topics. Difficulty was estimated using accepted solutions during the contest and results of teams that qualified for the Brazilian final. The levels were unsolved, very hard, hard, medium, and easy.

At least two ad-hoc problems appeared in every examined edition. Algorithmic paradigms, mathematics, and graphs were prominent. **Fifteen problems lacked precise topic classification**, including **ten in the 2022 and 2023 contests**, mainly because reliable solutions and editorials were scarce.

### Limitations, script credits, and next steps

Scoreboard data from the Natal site in 2015 could not be recovered. Extracting 2013–2016 results used scripts by **Fernando Kiotheka**; 2017–2023 extraction used scripts by **Ezequiel Soares da Silva**, adapted by the thesis author. The original thesis documents the methodology and limitations.

A suggested future project was a collection of contest editorials and solutions. The [current solution archive]({{ '/en/solutions/' | relative_url }}) takes a **separate educational step in that direction**, with other attributed problems and examples. It is not the thesis dataset and does not remove its original limitations.

Three documented examples show different techniques: [Kruskal with Union-Find]({{ '/en/solutions/kruskal/' | relative_url }}), [bottom-up dynamic programming]({{ '/en/solutions/bee1034-bottomup/' | relative_url }}), and [segment-tree lazy propagation]({{ '/en/solutions/bee1500/' | relative_url }}).
